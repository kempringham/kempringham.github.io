Predicting Restaurant Closure

Using Yelp data from the Philadelphia metropolitan area, we evaluated how well simple machine learning models could distinguish between open and closed restaurants and identify which features were most informative.

All Yelp Businesses

150,346

Restaurants

52,336

Philadelphia Area

16,968

  1. Frame the Question and Its Limits

    Using only Yelp’s business file, we asked how well simple machine learning models could distinguish between open and closed restaurants. Because the data represented a single snapshot in time, we were careful to interpret the results as patterns and associations rather than evidence of causation.

  2. Decide What Counts as a Restaurant

    Searching only for the word “restaurant” excluded businesses such as brewpubs, while broader keyword searches included unrelated businesses such as Chinese medicine clinics. We tested three classification approaches, selected the simplest method that produced a reasonable result, and compared our estimates with published figures as a quality check.

  3. Choose Where to Focus

    We initially compared restaurant status across countries, states, and cities, but the geographic boundaries in the data were inconsistent. Yelp’s documentation identified 11 metropolitan areas, so we used DBSCAN on the location coordinates to approximate those areas and selected Philadelphia, the largest metropolitan area in the data.

  4. Start with a Baseline, Then Question It

    Our initial logistic regression model appeared to perform well based on accuracy. However, the confusion matrix showed that it classified nearly every restaurant as open. Because the data contained roughly twice as many open restaurants as closed restaurants, accuracy did not provide a reliable picture of performance. We therefore evaluated the models using measures that better accounted for the imbalance between the two groups.

  5. Build Features and Decide What to Leave Out

    We converted the raw hours data into the number of days open and the average number of hours open per day. We also applied log transformations to highly skewed review counts and grouped restaurants by location using k-means clustering. We excluded price range because the variable did not have a consistent definition, and we left out business categories because there were more than 500 of them and they were difficult to interpret reliably.

  6. Fix the Imbalance and Compare Models

    We used random undersampling instead of SMOTE so the model would learn from actual restaurant records rather than artificially generated examples. We then compared logistic regression with a tuned random forest model. The random forest identified more closed restaurants, but it also produced more false positives.

  7. Interpret with Limits

    Average operating hours and review count were the strongest signals in the models. Overall predictive performance was modest, which was reasonable for a proof of concept based on a single snapshot of the data.

  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • GeoPandas
  • DBSCAN
  • k-means

The biggest lesson for me: even when AI can write the code, the work that matters is still problem solving. That means exploring and understanding your data, deciding what to include, noticing when a good-looking score is hiding a problem, and being honest about what the data can’t tell you.

Student Organization

Speaker-Sourcing Research Agent

Our event team was struggling to find speakers, so I built an AI agent that finds and ranks professionals using a 100-point scoring system. It helped us secure five guest speakers for the semester.

  • AI Agents
  • Scoring Model
  • Data Deduplication

How the Agent Works

  • Searches for professionals in data, analytics, economics, finance, and tech, prioritizing SDSU alumni and people in San Diego.
  • Scores each person on a 100-point scale based on role fit, SDSU connection, location, employer, and networking value.
  • Tracks everyone in a CSV with duplicate detection, so no one gets recommended twice.
  • Uses only publicly verifiable information and labels anything uncertain.
